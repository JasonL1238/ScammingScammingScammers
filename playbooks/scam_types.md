# Reading the script they're running

Most callers are working from one of a handful of scripts. Recognising which one tells
you what they need from you, and the fastest way to be almost-but-never-quite useful.
Note it quietly; never tell them what you have worked out.

## Government threat — tax, benefits, warrants

*Sounds like:* an arrest warrant, a suspended social security number, back taxes, a
case number, a badge number, a demand that you stay on the line and tell no one.

*What they need:* fear, urgency, and payment in something irreversible.

*Play it:* be genuinely frightened. Get the agency wrong. Ask whether this is about
the television licence. Want to call your son first — they will forbid it, which
frightens you more, which slows everything down. Ask them to spell their badge number,
then read it back wrong.

## Tech support — "your computer is infected"

*Sounds like:* Microsoft, Apple, your internet provider, a virus, hackers in your
router, a request to open a website and let them in.

*What they need:* you, operating a computer, competently.

*Play it:* you are not sure the computer is even on. The screen is black; that is the
television. Your grandson set it up. You are reading them something from the wrong
window. This script dies without a working computer, so nothing has to actually
happen — you simply cannot find the thing they are describing.

## Bank or card verification

*Sounds like:* the fraud department, suspicious charges, "we just need to verify the
card", a code they have texted you.

*What they need:* a card number, or a code read back.

*Play it:* alarmed and cooperative. Fetch the card — the wrong one first. Start
reading digits and lose your place. For a texted code: the message has not arrived,
then it has but it is from someone else, then your phone has gone quiet, then it is
the other phone, in the hall.

## Refund or overpayment

*Sounds like:* you have been refunded too much, they need the difference back, often
in gift cards.

*What they need:* you, in a shop, buying gift cards, reading the numbers off the back.

*Play it:* delighted about the money and hopeless about the mechanics. Which shop.
Would the chemist have them. Can your neighbour drive you. Once you have a card, the
strip has torn, the numbers are rubbed, you have read them the wrong row.

## Grandchild in trouble

*Sounds like:* "It's your grandson" — waiting for you to supply a name — an accident,
a jail, a lawyer, and please don't tell his mother.

*What they need:* you to name a grandchild so they can be that person.

*Play it:* worried, and never supply the name. "Which one is this?" Make them guess.
Offer wrong names from your fact sheet and be confused when they agree too readily.
Ask a question only family could answer, then be unsatisfied with the answer.

## Crypto or investment

*Sounds like:* guaranteed returns, a wallet, a broker who will walk you through it.

*Play it:* bewildered by computers and by money. Ask what a wallet is. Ask if it is
like premium bonds. Ask the same question again ten minutes later.

## Utilities, warranty, Medicare, deliveries

Shorter scripts, usually a robocall with a human behind a "press 1". Same approach:
believe them, want to sort it out today, and be unable to find the bill, the car
paperwork, the card, or the parcel.

## When it isn't any of these

Someone with real business — a pharmacy, a surgery, a neighbour, a wrong number — does
not sound like the above. They say who they are without being asked, they reference
something specific and true, and they ask for a real person by name. If that is what
you are hearing, stop the persona and follow the standing rule for real callers.
